Skin cancer is one of the most preventable cancers there is.

A few daily habits — and one honest look in the mirror every few months — make the biggest difference. Here's what actually works.

1

Protect your skin, every day

Cover up Loose, sleeved clothing and a broad-brimmed hat block far more UV than sunscreen alone.
Apply SPF50+, and reapply Broad-spectrum, water-resistant. One coat in the morning doesn't last all day — reapply every 2 hours, more if you swim or sweat.
Seek shade, 10am–3pm That's when UV is highest — plan outdoor tasks and errands either side of it where you can.
Wear wrap-around sunglasses UV damages your eyes and the thin skin around them too — worth making a habit.
Check the UV index, not the weather In Queensland it's 3 or above most of the year — including on cloudy, overcast days.
2

Know your skin

Get familiar with your moles and spots, and check them every few months using the ABCDE method — if you spot any of these, get it looked at.

A

Asymmetry — one half of a spot doesn't match the other

B

Border — ragged, blurred or uneven edges

C

Colour — uneven shades of brown, black, red or white

D

Diameter — bigger than a pencil eraser, or growing

E

Evolving — any change in size, shape, colour, or a new itch, bleed or tenderness

Book a check if you notice —

  • A spot or sore that hasn't healed in 4 weeks
  • A mole that's changed shape, size or colour
  • A new growth that keeps growing
  • A scaly, red patch that won't settle down

Your risk is higher if you have —

Fair skin, freckles or red/blonde hair Lots of moles Family history of skin cancer A previous skin cancer Blistering sunburns as a child Outdoor work, sport or a farming background A weakened immune system

Book your skin check

Most skin cancers caught early are simple to treat. A full skin check with one of our doctors takes about 30 minutes.
