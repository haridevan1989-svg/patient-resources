A few things before your skin check help us see everything.

None of this takes long — but it makes a real difference to how much we can find on the day.

1

Before you arrive

Come without makeup Even light coverage can hide the colour changes and borders we check for, especially on the face.
Remove nail polish Skin cancer can occur under and around nails — please remove polish from fingers and toes.
Wear your hair down We check the scalp too, so skip heavy hairspray or styling powders beforehand.
Hold off on fake tan Self-tanner changes how spots look — leave it 1–2 weeks before your appointment if you can.
Moisturise as normal, skip a heavy layer on the day Regular moisturising helps in the weeks before — a greasy layer on the day interferes with our equipment.
Wear something easy to change out of You'll be given a gown — simple, quick-to-remove clothing keeps things running smoothly.
2

What to bring & expect

A little detail beforehand and knowing what happens on the day both help the check run smoothly.

Good to bring or jot down

  • Photos or notes of any spots you're concerned about, and when you noticed them
  • Your own skin cancer history, if any
  • Family history of melanoma or skin cancer
  • A rough idea of your sun exposure or sunburn history

What to expect on the day

  • Usually 10–20 minutes — longer if you have a lot of moles or a prior history
  • We check scalp to soles, including areas not usually sun-exposed
  • A dermatoscope (magnified light) is used on any spots of interest
  • Intimate areas are only ever examined with your consent

Why it's worth the prep

A clear, unobstructed check gives us the best chance of finding something early — while it's still simple to treat.

Noticed a spot that's changed, bleeding, or won't heal? Mention it to reception when you arrive so we make sure it's looked at closely.

Questions before your visit?

Call us anytime before your appointment — we're happy to help you get ready.
